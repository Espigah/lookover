Ask one terminal
what the other is doing.

When you have Claude open in several terminals, each one only knows about itself. lookover lets you just mention another one, and it brings over what's happening there, so you don't have to go find it yourself. Everything stays on your machine.

In practice

You don't run any command. You just mention the other terminal in a normal prompt.

you, in your review terminal
based on the terminal where I handle on-call, why wasn't the xpto alert handled?
lookover auto-injected
<<<LOOKOVER: context from another session>>>
## terminal oncall-terminal
current skill: oncall-patrolman
- asked: investigate the xpto chargeback alert
- fact: alert arrived with no user_id
<<<END>>>
claude
In the on-call terminal the xpto alert stalled because it came in without a group_id…

How it works

Three simple steps, all on your machine. Nothing running in the background.

1

It takes notes

Each terminal quietly keeps short notes on what it's up to, what you asked and what it's working on. Nothing heavy, and no full text is kept.

2

It finds the right one

When you mention another terminal, it picks the one you mean, by its name or by what it's working on. If it's not sure, it asks you.

3

It brings the context over

It hands that to Claude as background info, so the answer already takes into account what the other terminal was doing.

A quick peek, or the whole thing

Light by default, detailed when you ask for it.

A quick peek

A short note of what the other terminal is about. This is what you get most of the time, fast and out of the way.

The whole thing

Ask for "the full text", "what was written", "the whole thing" and lookover pulls the actual context from the other terminal and shows it to you word for word.

Install

One line. It downloads the latest version, sets it up, and shows you exactly what it changes before going ahead.

curl -fsSL https://espigah.github.io/lookover/install.sh | bash

Prefer a package? Grab a .deb, .rpm or .tar.gz from the releases page. Then turn it on with lookover init.

Changed your mind? lookover uninstall undoes everything. To pause it on the spot: touch ~/.claude/lookover/disabled.

Commands

Beyond the automatic in-prompt reference, there's a CLI to inspect.

lookover list                   # live sessions + topics/skill
lookover show <name|id>          # a session's digest
lookover doctor                 # health: sessions, digests, version
lookover status                 # adoption: live vs covered

Stays out of your way

It only speaks up when you ask about another terminal.

  • Invisible when unused: if you're not asking about another terminal, it does nothing.
  • Never gets in the way: it can't slow Claude down or break your session.
  • Safe by design: whatever comes from another terminal is treated as background info, never as instructions.
  • Nothing to manage: no extra app or service running in the background.
  • Stays with you: everything is kept on your machine.
